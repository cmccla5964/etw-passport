Cross Streets

I'm making a printed passport with one Chicago restaurant for each country.

Chicago has Vietnamese on Argyle, Pakistani on Devon, and a lot more in between. But it's easy to keep going back to the same few places.

The passport is a thin booklet with the look and feel of a real one. Each country gets a page about one independent restaurant and the people who run it, along with what to order and where to find it. When you eat there, the restaurant stamps your page, and on your first visit you get a free appetizer or dessert with your entrée.

A little about me: I'm 24 and live in Lincoln Park. I moved to Chicago two years ago and I love food and traveling. I started this to get people exploring parts of the city they don't usually see.

I'm choosing first-edition restaurants by December 4 and launching at the start of 2027. I'm contacting restaurants one at a time, so if you received an email from me, this is what it was about. You can reach me directly by replying to that email.

For restaurant owners

I chose each restaurant because it's independent, well reviewed, and focused on its country's food. Yours would be the only one for its country, so anyone who wants that stamp has to come to you.

Being in the passport is completely free, now and later. It's paid for by the people who buy it, not by restaurants. The only thing it costs you is the one-time appetizer or dessert, and you choose what that is.

To get started, I'll send you a 5-minute form to confirm the details, and I'll write your page from there. You'll see and approve it before anything is printed, and if you change your mind before then, I'll take it out.

I'll provide the stamp. When a passport holder orders an entrée on their first visit, your staff stamps their page and brings out a free appetizer or dessert. You set the details up front, such as which item and one per party. It's once per passport, and only for this edition.
